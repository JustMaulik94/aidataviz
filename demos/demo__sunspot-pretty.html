Sunspot Butterfly: Austere vs. Beautiful

Two valid Tufte interpretations of the same data — one maximally spare, one maximally rich.

"Graphical elegance is often found in simplicity of design and complexity of data."
— Edward Tufte, The Visual Display of Quantitative Information

Version A — Maximally Austere

Dot-dash plot. Range frame. No color — only position and density encode the data.

Every dot is one sunspot group observation. No color, no interpolation — only x (time), y (latitude), and density of overlap. The butterfly wings emerge from the data itself. This version follows the strict Tufte dictum: maximize data-ink, eliminate everything else.

Version B — Beautiful Evidence

Dark background. Ridge-line profiles per cycle. Color as data. Tufte's "Beautiful Evidence" aesthetic.

The same butterfly diagram on a dark ground. Ridge fills show the latitudinal spread of each active band per year; individual observations plot as warm-toned dots. The dark background is not decoration — it raises the contrast of the faint early cycles (low SSN) against the bright active maxima, making weak signal visible that washes out on white.

What the skill changed — and why two versions

  • Austere version. Strict data-ink ratio maximization: only position encodes the data. No color, no fill, no border. The butterfly pattern is pure geometry emerging from dot density.
  • Beautiful version. Dark background raises contrast for sparse early cycles. Color encodes latitude continuously (yellow = high-lat, orange = mid-lat, purple = equatorial) — this is a multifunctioning element: the dots encode both position and latitude class simultaneously.
  • Ridge fills (Beautiful only). Translucent orange fills show the extent of each active latitude band per year, giving a macro reading of the band width without hiding the individual dots underneath.
  • Both versions avoid chartjunk. No drop shadows, no border box, no color gradient fills on non-data elements, no decorative axes.
  • Both versions use range frame. Axis lines span only the latitude range where data exists. No full border box.
  • Dark is not decoration. In the Beautiful version, the dark background is justified: weak signals (low-SSN cycles 12, 14, 16, 24) are more visible against dark than light because the dot opacity is calibrated to cycle intensity, and the dark field provides maximum contrast for the faintest observations.
  • Inline cycle labels. "C12" through "C24" annotate each cycle directly at the top of its column. No legend, no separate list.
